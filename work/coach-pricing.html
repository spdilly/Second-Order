Pricing seats and departures

A route-level analysis of advance fares, displaced demand, and the cost of adding capacity.

A discount changes the passenger mixMore advance bookings can reduce contributionExtra capacity must earn its operating cost

A discount changes the passenger mix

Advance discounts attract more price-sensitive demand, but passengers compete for the same seats. On a busy departure, early sales can replace later passengers paying the full fare. On a quieter departure, additional early sales can fill capacity that would otherwise depart empty.

Under these assumptions, the best tested off-peak discount is 36%. A busy departure requires a separate inventory decision: protecting 30 late-booking seats limits displacement, although an excessively large reserve could leave seats empty if the late-demand forecast is wrong.

More advance bookings can reduce contribution

A 20% discount increases early bookings from 32.0 to 45.7. The departure is already full, so late bookings decline from 18.0 to 4.3. Passenger volume and passenger-related costs are unchanged.

The bridge separates revenue from additional advance bookings, the lower fare applied to all advance tickets, and displaced late sales. Counting only the additional advance bookings would overstate the benefit.

Extra capacity must earn its operating cost

A second departure removes the original seat constraint but adds a separate trip cost. The sensitivity compares one and two departures at a 15% advance discount, protecting higher-fare late demand in both cases.

The positive cells identify combinations where incremental ticket contribution covers the second trip. The calculation assumes passengers accept the added departure time and demand is known in advance; operational approval would also require equipment, drivers, and a credible booking forecast.

Worked scenario using the assumptions below. Figures demonstrate the analysis and do not represent a realized client result.

Model notes and assumptions
  • One departure has 50 seats; undiscounted fare is $43 for both segments.
  • Busy reference demand is 32 advance and 30 late passengers; off-peak demand is 18 advance and 12 late passengers. Fractional expected passengers are allowed.
  • Advance demand follows constant elasticity of -1.6 against the $43 reference fare; late demand and fare do not change.
  • One departure costs $900; passenger servicing costs $3 per passenger. Heatmap varies the incremental second-departure cost from $600 to $1,400.
  • Protected-seat scenarios reserve up to forecast late demand. Capacity comparison assumes perfect demand foresight and passenger acceptance of either departure.

All numerical inputs are assumptions, not measured client results or externally verified market facts.

No demand uncertainty, no-shows, competitor response, taxes, depot overhead, vehicle capital charge, or schedule-specific demand penalty. Contribution is not company net profit.

One route/departure illustration cannot be extrapolated to the previously published $41.8M network lift.
