Testing the assumptions embedded in Constellation's share price.

CONSTELLATION ENERGY · JUNE 2026 RESEARCH SAMPLE

IN THIS CASE

The questionThe analysisPolicy supportThe research note

This research sample examines whether Constellation Energy's valuation already reflected the expected growth in long-term nuclear power contracts. The linked note is dated June 2026. Prices, operating assumptions and valuation outputs below refer to that analysis.

The question

Long-term contracts can reduce exposure to wholesale power prices. The investment question is how much of that benefit the share price already assumes. The note tests contract volumes and prices alongside the discount rate applied to future cash flows.

The analysis

The reverse discounted cash flow supported the note's $255 reference share price at a 0.90 beta and an 8.1 percent weighted average cost of capital. Using a beta of 1.0 to 1.2 produced values of $218 to $241 per share. Under those assumptions, the reference price already incorporated a lower level of equity risk.

The note also compares assumed contract growth with disclosed volumes and tests the value of premium power contracts, capacity revenue and interconnection rights. These comparisons identify the operating evidence that would justify changing the valuation.

Policy support

The federal section 45U nuclear production credit can support qualifying generation when receipts are lower. The credit is reduced as facility gross receipts rise and is subject to eligibility conditions. The note models it as downside support through 2032. IRS credit guidance.

The research note

The fourteen-slide note includes the reverse DCF, beta sensitivity, contract-volume and pricing scenarios, and the evidence that would change the conclusion.
