Childcare capacity and debt coverage

The economics of the next classroom

Enrollment creates revenue one child at a time. Staffing costs arrive in steps, and age mix changes where those steps fall.

The next enrollment can reduce cash coverage

Annual cash available for debt includes a maintenance reserve. Each staffing step can temporarily outweigh the revenue from an additional child; a smooth occupancy assumption hides that exposure.

Higher reimbursement comes with a staffing tradeoff

The younger-child rate is higher in this scenario, but its staffing assumption is twice as intensive. The mix with the most revenue need not produce the most cash. Physical capacity alone does not establish usable licensed capacity.

Debt is paid from the residual cash

At 60 enrolled children and a 30% younger mix, the bridge separates tuition receipts, staffing, operating costs, reserves and debt. It uses the same assumptions as the sensitivity curves.

Worked scenario using the assumptions below. Figures demonstrate the analysis and do not represent a realized client result.

Model notes and assumptions
  • 72-child physical capacity; 36-72 enrolled children; age counts rounded to nearest whole child using Python round.
  • Annual receipts $24,000 younger / $18,000 older; fully collected, no lag or absence deduction.
  • One staff position per four younger or eight older children, rounded up independently; analytical staffing assumptions, not legal ratios.
  • $52,000 annual loaded cost per staff position; $2,200 per child operating cost; $280,000 fixed operating costs; $24,000 annual maintenance reserve; $120,000 annual debt service.
  • Coverage is cash before debt after the maintenance reserve divided by debt service; no lender-specific threshold is asserted.

No verified room allocation, licensing, wage schedule or reimbursement program is modeled.

Steady-state annual model excludes enrollment timing, receivable delays, taxes and financing fees.
