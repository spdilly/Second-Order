Earnings at sale. Cash after closing.

A buyer can accept the earnings calculation and still need more cash to fund the business. Collection terms connect the two.

An adjustment has to survive the next owner.

The seller proposes $4.38m of adjusted EBITDA. Recurring temporary labor accounts for $0.25m of the proposed adjustments; the buyer would continue paying it. Removing that item leaves $4.13m before the cash requirements of the business are considered.

Growth raises earnings before it releases cash.

A 25% increase in revenue lifts EBITDA to $5.16m. If collections also move from 55 to 72 days, the additional receivables absorb $1.63m. The same sales and operating margin then produce $0.48m of annual cash after tax, maintenance spending, and working-capital investment.

A purchase financed against EBITDA needs a separate forecast for cash. Otherwise the buyer can meet the valuation case and still arrive at closing with too little liquidity.

The collection cycle changes the funding decision.

Higher growth funds more receivables at the same time that it earns additional margin. The grid varies growth and collection days together. It identifies the combinations that require cash even though the income statement remains profitable.

The working-capital agreement reaches the seller’s proceeds.

At a 6.0× multiple, $4.13m of maintainable EBITDA implies $24.78m of enterprise value. If the additional receivables are collectible and included in closing working capital, the $1.63m surplus increases seller proceeds. The buyer funds that payment while waiting longer to collect the cash.

The example holds the multiple fixed and values the receivables at face value. Disputed or uncollectible balances would require a separate adjustment; slow collection alone does not establish a seller debit.

Worked scenario using the assumptions below. Figures demonstrate the analysis and do not represent a realized client result.

Model notes and assumptions
  • Opening revenue $28m; reported EBITDA $3.20m. Accepted adjustments are $0.40m legal settlement, $0.35m owner compensation, and $0.18m ERP implementation. A $0.25m recurring labor adjustment is rejected.
  • Normalized EBITDA margin is held at 14.75%. Cost of goods sold is 64% of sales; inventory and payable days are 20 and 30. Opening receivable days are 55.
  • Maintenance capex is $0.80m and depreciation $0.55m at opening revenue, each growing with sales. Cash tax is 25% of positive EBIT; no interest, growth capex, or tax loss carryforward is included.
  • Cash available = EBITDA − cash tax − maintenance capex − increase in net working capital. All cases represent a single forward year.
  • Sale value uses a fixed 6.0× multiple, $8.40m net debt, $0.65m fees, and a working-capital target at 55 collection days on $35m sales. The seller-cash bridge adds actual working capital above the target; all receivables are assumed collectible and included at face value.

No client outcome is represented. Add-back acceptance, financing, and closing adjustments depend on the actual evidence and agreement.
